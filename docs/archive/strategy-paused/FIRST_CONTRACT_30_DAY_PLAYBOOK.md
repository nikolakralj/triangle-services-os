# Triangle Services: First-Contract 30-Day Playbook

**Companion to:** `CONTRACT_FIRST_STRATEGY_REVIEW_2026-08-28.md`

**Objective:** Produce a qualified commercial requirement, a written proposal, and a credible path to one signed supplier agreement/job order. Activity targets are management targets, not forecasts.

**Adopted as the Phase 0 operating playbook:** 29 August 2026. Current ordered
execution and gate evidence live in `../../ROADMAP_EXECUTION.md`.

## The only objective

> Win permission to supply a real, legally deliverable technical crew or specialist team to a real buyer at positive contribution margin.

For 30 days, do not optimize the AI workforce interface, add agents, expand sectors, or collect more generic project signals.

## Days 1–3: choose what Triangle can truthfully sell

### 1. Reconcile supply

Contact every worker/subcontractor who may be part of the first package. Record:

- exact trade and current competence;
- availability date and commitment confidence;
- countries accepted;
- languages;
- expected rate;
- employment/subcontract relationship;
- passport/right-to-work situation;
- A1 feasibility;
- certificates and expiries;
- driving/tools/accommodation needs;
- recent relevant reference;
- supervisor/foreman capability.

Availability older than 14 days is unconfirmed.

### 2. Select one package

Choose one:

- supervised electrical installation/fit-out crew; or
- PCS7/automation/commissioning team.

Select from evidence, not ambition. The current system has three available electrical workers but the strongest inbox demand is automation. Resolve that mismatch before selling.

### 3. Write the package card

One page only:

```text
Package name
Scope included
Scope excluded
Roles and headcount available
Supervisor
Earliest mobilization date
Target countries
Languages
Relevant references
Documents ready / missing
Commercial model
Rate assumptions
Client inputs required
```

## Days 2–5: make the package contract-ready

### Legal/commercial review

For the selected first country and buyer type, confirm with a qualified adviser:

- works subcontract vs labor leasing vs agency/referral;
- licensing requirements;
- posting declaration and A1 process;
- local pay/collective-agreement rules;
- working time/overtime;
- insurance;
- tax and permanent-establishment exposure;
- acceptable worker relationship;
- required contract clauses.

### Landed-cost sheet

Calculate:

```text
bill rate
- worker cost
- statutory/employer cost
- travel/accommodation/per diem
- supervision and non-productive hours
- PPE/tools/training/certification
- insurance/compliance/admin
- financing/FX/bad-debt allowance
= contribution margin
```

Set:

- target price;
- minimum price;
- maximum payment term;
- overtime/shift/travel assumptions;
- walk-away conditions;
- maximum payroll funding exposure.

### Supplier-readiness pack

Prepare a single folder containing company, insurance, HSE, quality, compliance, banking, references, capability, and worker-readiness material. Do not send worker personal data before there is a lawful purpose and an appropriate recipient.

## Week 1: work the demand already in the building

The live system contains four leads scoring 70+ and three drafts, but no sent reply is recorded.

For each high-scoring lead:

1. Read the original message.
2. Check whether it matches the selected package.
3. Send a human-written/approved reply from the normal mailbox.
4. Ask whether the client accepts a supplier team instead of only an individual freelancer.
5. Ask only the missing fields needed to qualify the requirement.
6. Request a 15-minute call.
7. Record the sent date and next follow-up date.

Qualification questions:

- Who is the end client or delivery contractor?
- Who signs the supplier/job order?
- Is this individual augmentation or a managed team/scope?
- How many people are needed now and over the next 90 days?
- What exact scope, technology, and seniority?
- Where, when, for how long, and on which shift pattern?
- What engagement model is permitted?
- What rate/budget range and payment term?
- What supplier onboarding and site documents are required?
- What is the decision process and next date?

## Week 2: use invited supplier routes

Start with:

1. Mercury Engineering supply-chain/labor-agency registration.
2. Exyte supplier/prequalification route, linked to current Frankfurt activity.
3. SPIE MySourcing and the relevant German operating unit.
4. Bilfinger entity-specific supplier/subcontractor registration.
5. Existing recruiter/agency relationships in the inbox.

For each account, record:

- target entity/country;
- current project or demand evidence;
- correct procurement route;
- likely buyer role;
- delivery model they accept;
- prequalification requirements;
- introduction/submission date;
- human owner;
- next action/date.

The initial objective is a procurement or workforce-supply conversation, not a mass email.

## Weeks 2–3: build a 25-account strike list

Use five groups of five accounts:

- data-center MEP/electrical contractors;
- high-tech EPC/general contractors;
- industrial services/maintenance contractors;
- engineering staffing/framework partners;
- current project award winners relevant to the package.

Every account must have:

- a specific reason it may need the package;
- evidence dated within the last 12 months;
- the actual contracting entity;
- a buyer or procurement route;
- country/legal feasibility;
- one next human action.

An account without those fields is research backlog, not a target.

## Weeks 3–4: convert conversations into commercial objects

### A lead becomes qualified only when

- the buyer/agent confirms real demand;
- Triangle's engagement model is acceptable;
- scope, headcount, timing, location, and duration are sufficiently known;
- rate/budget logic exists;
- supplier onboarding is feasible;
- Triangle has credible crew coverage;
- a dated next step exists.

### A proposal must include

- buyer and project/site;
- scope and exclusions;
- crew composition;
- mobilization date;
- supervision;
- rate and time assumptions;
- travel/accommodation responsibility;
- timesheet/acceptance process;
- required documents;
- validity period;
- dependencies;
- proposed contracting route;
- next decision date.

## Decision at day 30

### Continue this package if

- buyers accept the delivery model;
- at least one concrete requirement/vendor process exists;
- Triangle can deliver at positive contribution margin;
- compliance and funding are feasible.

### Change the package if

- buyers respond but need a different skill/headcount;
- demand repeatedly maps to a capability Triangle can realistically assemble;
- the current package cannot meet site or commercial requirements.

### Change the market/channel if

- the package is strong but the buyer route is blocked;
- the country makes the intended engagement model impractical;
- supplier qualification excludes Triangle at its current maturity.

### Do not automate if

- manual outreach does not convert;
- supplier documentation is incomplete;
- worker availability is untrustworthy;
- the legal model is unresolved;
- the offer is still “we can provide manpower.”
