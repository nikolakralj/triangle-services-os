# Problem interview kit

**Prepared:** 4 September 2026  
**Queue item:** GTM-002  
**Status:** dormant reference; client search paused by management 4 September 2026  
**Audience:** owners, managing directors, operations leaders, and senior
recruiters at European technical staffing, crew-supply, and labour-
subcontracting businesses.

## What this interview must prove

The interview is not a product demo and a positive reaction is not evidence.
It should establish whether comparable operators repeatedly experience a
costly, urgent problem moving real work through this chain:

```text
signal or confirmed supply
-> qualified requirement
-> buyer/procurement route
-> truthful crew or specialist package
-> human commercial action
-> order
-> mobilisation and delivery
-> invoice, payment, and margin
```

Phase 0 customer evidence requires:

- 12 qualified interviews;
- at least five independent operators describing the same costly workflow
  problem from recent experience;
- at least three concrete design-partner commitments, such as a real-data
  workshop, pilot LOI, or paid scoped pilot.

Compliments, feature requests without a recent example, mailing-list interest,
and hypothetical willingness to pay do not satisfy those gates.

## Participant mix

Aim for 12 interviews across more than one operating perspective:

| Role | Minimum | What they know |
|---|---:|---|
| Owner / managing director | 4 | Commercial model, risk, buying authority, margin, priorities |
| Operations / compliance leader | 3 | Cross-border onboarding, mobilisation, timesheets, exceptions |
| Recruitment / delivery lead | 3 | Demand intake, qualification, matching, candidate/crew truth |
| Finance / commercial lead | 2 | Rates, funding exposure, invoicing, payment, realised margin |

One person may cover multiple roles in a small firm, but record which hat they
were wearing. Prefer firms close to the ICP; use larger agencies only as
comparators and label them as such.

## Before the call

1. A human approves the company, participant, and contact channel.
2. Record why the firm fits and which public source supports that view.
3. Prepare a neutral one-sentence reason for the interview: learning how
   technical staffing and crew operators run contract-to-mobilisation work.
4. Do not send a deck or feature list before the interview.
5. Ask permission before recording. If permission is not given, take notes.
6. Do not request worker CVs, candidate data, customer contracts, passwords,
   or other confidential material.

## 40-minute interview guide

### 1. Context — 5 minutes

- What kind of technical work do you supply: individual contractors, managed
  crews, subcontracted scope, permanent hires, or a mix?
- Which sectors and countries make up most of the work today?
- Roughly how many people touch a live requirement between first signal and
  mobilisation?
- Which part of that process are you personally responsible for?

Do not ask whether they have the problem. Ask how they work.

### 2. Reconstruct the last real case — 15 minutes

“Please take the most recent technical requirement or crew request you worked
on—not the ideal process. What happened from the moment it arrived?”

Follow the timeline:

- Where did the demand come from?
- What did you know at first, and what was missing?
- How did you establish who could actually buy or issue the order?
- How did you confirm scope, headcount, timing, location, shifts, engagement
  model, rate/budget, and supplier requirements?
- How did you determine who was genuinely available and ready?
- Where were documents, conversations, approvals, and versions stored?
- What did you send, who approved it, and how was follow-up remembered?
- What changed between shortlist, submission, acceptance, and mobilisation?
- How were timesheets, invoice, payment, and margin connected back to the
  original requirement?

For every handoff ask: “What was copied, re-entered, checked again, or lost?”

### 3. Pain, frequency, and consequence — 10 minutes

For each concrete failure or workaround:

- When did this last happen?
- How often does it happen in a normal month?
- Who spends time fixing it, and roughly how much?
- Did it delay a shortlist, order, mobilisation, invoice, or payment?
- Did it create an incorrect claim, duplicated approach, unavailable worker,
  compliance risk, rate/margin error, or missed follow-up?
- What happened commercially: lost fee, idle worker, payroll funding, write-
  off, client frustration, or no measurable consequence?
- What do you do today to prevent it?
- What have you already tried, paid for, or replaced?

Ask for ranges when exact figures are sensitive. Record unknown rather than
inventing a number.

### 4. Decision and change behaviour — 7 minutes

- Which systems are the source of truth today? Which ones are ignored?
- Who can change the workflow or buy software?
- What would make a new system too risky to use with live candidates and
  clients?
- Which data would have to import on day one, and what must export if the pilot
  ends?
- Which actions must always remain human-approved?
- What measurable result would justify a paid pilot within 30–60 days?
- Is this important enough to change this quarter? What competes with it?

### 5. Close — 3 minutes

Summarise the strongest observed problem in the participant's language and
ask whether that summary is accurate.

If the interview contains a repeated, costly problem and a credible sponsor,
ask for one concrete next step:

- a second workflow-mapping session;
- a real-data workshop using anonymised or synthetic structure first;
- review of a one-page paid pilot scope;
- a pilot LOI; or
- a paid scoped pilot discussion.

Do not convert politeness into commitment. Record the exact action, owner, and
date, or record `none`.

## Pain and cost worksheet

Use ranges and show the participant's basis. Do not present a calculated value
as audited financial truth.

| Pain event | Frequency/month | People involved | Extra hours/event | Loaded hourly cost | Direct cost/event | Delay days | Revenue/margin at risk | Confidence | Source words |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---|---|
|  |  |  |  |  |  |  |  |  |  |

Useful calculations:

```text
monthly rework cost
= frequency × people × extra hours × loaded hourly cost

monthly direct loss
= frequency × direct cost per event

estimated monthly exposure
= rework cost + direct loss + separately evidenced delay or margin exposure
```

Keep `measured`, `participant estimate`, and `researcher inference` separate.
Do not add revenue-at-risk to direct loss unless the participant confirms it
was actually lost; otherwise label it exposure.

## Pilot qualification score

Score each dimension `0`, `1`, or `2`. Add the evidence beside the score.

| Dimension | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| ICP fit | Outside technical contract staffing/crew supply | Adjacent or much larger/smaller | European 2–25-person technical staffing/crew operator |
| Recent pain | Hypothetical | Example older than six months | Concrete example in last 90 days |
| Frequency | Rare/one-off | Quarterly | Monthly or more often |
| Consequence | No material effect | Time/friction only | Lost/delayed order, mobilisation, cash, margin, or trust |
| Workflow breadth | One isolated task | Two to three connected stages | Crosses demand, supply, commercial action, and delivery |
| Existing workaround | No process and no urgency | Manual but tolerated | Active spend/rework or failed tool replacement |
| Sponsor | No owner | Interested user without authority | Named owner/MD/operations sponsor |
| Data readiness | Cannot provide safe test data | Can provide synthetic/anonymised structure | Can provide authorised real pilot data with safeguards |
| Measurable pilot result | No success definition | General time saving | Baseline and 30–60 day outcome metric available |
| Commitment | No next step | Another conversation | Dated workshop, LOI, or paid pilot discussion |

Interpretation:

- `16–20`: strong design-partner candidate; prepare a scoped paid pilot only
  if all mandatory gates below pass.
- `11–15`: continue discovery; close evidence gaps before proposing a pilot.
- `0–10`: do not pursue now; retain as research evidence or comparator.

Mandatory gates regardless of score:

- a responsible human owns candidate/client data and can authorise its use;
- external messages, commercial commitments, worker decisions, and personal-
  data sharing remain human-controlled;
- the sponsor accepts a defined scope, success metric, export/exit path, and
  paid-pilot conversation;
- the requested work fits the contract-to-crew product rather than requiring a
  generic CRM, job board, autonomous outreach engine, or marketplace.

## Evidence log

Use [the CSV template](./problem-interview-evidence-log.csv) as the running
cross-interview ledger. One row represents one claim or observed event, not one
whole call.

Required evidence types:

- `observed_recent_case` — participant reconstructs an actual recent event;
- `participant_estimate` — stated frequency, time, or cost range;
- `artifact_seen` — authorised view of a workflow artifact, with no sensitive
  copy retained unless explicitly permitted;
- `researcher_inference` — interpretation that must remain labelled;
- `commitment` — exact next action, owner, and date.

## Cross-interview decision rule

After interviews 4, 8, and 12, group evidence by problem—not by requested
feature. A repeated pain counts only when separate companies describe the same
underlying failure with a recent example. Preserve contrary evidence and firms
that do not experience the problem.

Advance to GTM-003 only when at least five qualified interviews repeat the
same costly problem and one target requests a concrete next step. The resulting
pilot scope must be built from that evidence; it must not be a disguised plan
to implement Triangle's existing backlog.
