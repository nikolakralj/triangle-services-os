# Design-partner target list

**Research date:** 4 September 2026  
**Queue item:** GTM-001  
**Status:** dormant reference; client search paused by management 4 September 2026  
**Purpose:** prepare—not contact—European technical staffing, crew-supply,
and labour-subcontracting businesses for founder-led problem interviews.

## Selection criteria

The target customer is a 2–25-person European operator whose work crosses
several of Triangle's core states: technical demand, contractor or crew
availability, compliance/mobilisation, buyer communication, placement, and
delivery. Preference goes to firms that publicly describe contract or project
staffing, technical trades, mobile teams, cross-border delivery, or a tightly
defined engineering niche.

Public headcount is rarely disclosed. A `verified` size signal below is only
what the firm's own site explicitly shows (for example, two founders or five
consultants); it is not a claim about total payroll. `Unverified` means the
size must be checked before the firm is treated as inside the 2–25-person ICP.

The likely buyer is deliberately a **role hypothesis**, not a person or
contact record. No outreach, form submission, or personal-data enrichment was
performed.

## Recommended interview order

Start with rows 1–12. They combine a narrow technical vertical with visible
founder or director involvement and the workflow complexity Triangle is meant
to solve. Within that group, prioritise ROA Talent, DRS, Inova, BKCS, PRS, and
Green Scout because their own sites give the clearest evidence of a small,
operator-led team.

Before any contact, a human should verify current headcount, ownership, and
country, then approve the company and channel. The problem interview should
test the workflow rather than pitch features: how the firm moves from demand
or available people to a qualified requirement, compliant engagement route,
crew/package, human send, mobilisation, and margin—and where information is
lost or re-entered.

## Evidence limitations

- Sources are company-controlled public pages, so service and performance
  claims are positioning evidence, not independent proof.
- Headcount is unknown for most firms and must not be inferred from website
  design, contact count, or language such as `boutique`.
- A published contract service does not prove the firm supplies full crews;
  several targets may primarily place individual contractors. That distinction
  is an interview question.
- Inclusion is research preparation, not endorsement, qualification, or
  permission to contact.
